# Operator revision boundary

This record captures only the observable changes in the approved operator revision of `sculpture-finish-guide` (2026-08-20). It is a change log and a boundary for future ORS work, not a universal writing template or proof that the operator wants every article to use the same structure.

## Observed changes in this revision

The operator:

- changed the SEO title and H1 from a general “choose the right surface” framing to a concrete list of the materials actually compared: bronze, stainless steel, stone, and fiberglass;
- replaced the generic section `Custom Sculpture Finish Approval Process: From Sample to Production` with the Origin-attributed section `How Origin Sculpture Controls a Finish from Physical Sample to Final Inspection`;
- reorganized that section around environment, physical reference, production/QC, and installation/maintenance;
- changed wording from general advice to specific customer-facing review points, including substrate/system, finish sequence, visual range, seams and edges, acceptable variation, coating, cleaning, inspection, and repair points;
- added the limitation that a small flat sample does not guarantee identical color or reflection across a large curved form;
- replaced the more specific phrase `inspection interval` with the narrower `inspection points`;
- changed `composite system` to `fiberglass system` in the context of this article;
- used qualified wording such as `may cover` and `support review` for process claims.

## Boundary: what must not be inferred

- Do not force this exact title pattern, section heading, or four-stage sequence into unrelated topics.
- Do not treat this single revision as evidence that every article needs more Origin-branded process content, more subsections, or a physical sample discussion.
- Do not convert the operator's wording into a claim that Origin universally performs every listed activity. Each company-specific statement still needs current Origin evidence or user-provided evidence.
- Do not add maintenance intervals, material grades, warranties, repair promises, project results, or service scope merely because the revised article mentions inspection or repair points.
- Do not assume a wording change is a new business rule. `fiberglass system`, `inspection points`, and similar terms apply only where they are accurate for the topic and supported by the source material.
- Do not treat the operator's changes as permission to change the handle, publication status, images, internal-link plan, or Shopify record unless the operator explicitly requests those changes.

When another operator revision is supplied, compare it against the prior approved version, record only repeated or explicitly confirmed patterns, and keep one-off edits in the revision record rather than promoting them to a global rule.

## Operator revision: Custom Sculpture Process (2026-08-28)

### Observed changes in this revision

The operator's approved revision of `custom-sculpture-process`:

- revised the title and opening from a broad production overview into a buyer-facing step-by-step guide, and made the initial brief and preliminary quotation explicit;
- expanded the article's process from four steps to eight by separating material/finish/base approval, technical and installation coordination, packing/international delivery, and handover;
- added the scope caveat that a preliminary cost can change when the design, structure, custom base, packing, shipping, installation, or another scoped item changes;
- clarified the article's installation boundary: the buyer's local contractor prepares the site, equipment, and local approvals, while Origin Sculpture does not provide on-site installation;
- presented base plate and below-grade reinforced-frame concepts as context-dependent connection options, not universal specifications; final requirements depend on the sculpture, site, loads, concealment goal, local requirements, and destination professionals;
- added material-specific production concepts and stated that natural or hand-finished variation remains possible;
- separated sample approval from final acceptance: samples can establish colour, gloss, and general texture, but do not reproduce every curved reflection, bronze tone, or stone vein;
- added project-dependent packing, transport, customs, delivery, and receiving considerations; and
- changed generic installation/process wording to conditional scope language such as `may`, `depending on scope`, `subject to feasibility`, and `according to project scope`.

### Limited reusable signals

For a custom-sculpture, installation, delivery, or buyer-coordination article, and only when supported by current sources or explicit operator input:

- orient the opening around the buyer decisions that affect the project, such as the initial brief, provisional scope, approvals, site coordination, delivery, and handover;
- present material/finish, connection, production/QC, packing, delivery, and local-site responsibilities as relevant possible decision points, not as a mandatory fixed sequence; and
- qualify preliminary pricing, natural/hand-finished variation, technical connection concepts, installation responsibility, and local requirements by the actual project scope, site, load conditions, applicable codes, and verified service boundary.

### Boundary: what must not be inferred

- Do not require every custom-sculpture article to use the eight-step structure, the title wording, or all of the listed decision points.
- Do not state that Origin Sculpture never provides on-site installation as a site-wide fact unless the current article has verified, operator-approved business evidence for that exact claim.
- Do not turn base plates, embedded frames, anchors, embedment, grout, foundations, local-professional review, AISC/OSHA references, or material-process sequences into universal technical specifications.
- Do not add fixed packing, shipping, customs, receiving, maintenance, inspection, warranty, repair, pricing, or delivery commitments from this single revision.
- Do not infer a new internal-link count, anchor-text rule, image/alt-text standard, Blog Filter, handle rule, or Shopify permission from the presence of those elements in the revision.

### Revision checklist for this topic family

Before accepting a custom-process or installation draft, ask:

1. Does the article identify buyer decisions without making all projects follow one process?
2. Are pricing, site, connection, installation, delivery, and handover statements limited to verified scope and evidence?
3. Are technical examples presented as conditional concepts rather than specifications?
4. Are sample approval, natural variation, and final acceptance clearly distinguished where relevant?
5. Does every Origin-specific service boundary have a current source or explicit operator confirmation?

## Limited reusable signals

### 1. Title specificity

- Prefer the material names and decision terms a buyer is likely to use, such as `bronze`, `stainless steel`, `stone`, and `fiberglass`.
- Replace abstract wording such as “choose the right surface” when the article is actually comparing named materials and finishes.
- Keep the public H1 natural and readable. Use `and` in the H1 when it reads better than an ampersand; reserve compact punctuation for the SEO title when useful.
- Make the SEO title, H1, and opening promise describe the same comparison. Do not change the handle merely to accommodate a title refinement.

### 2. Evidence-led process copy when the topic supports it

- When the article has verified Origin evidence, replace a generic heading such as `Custom ... Process` with a topic-specific heading that names the decision or control point and attributes it to Origin.
- The section should explain what a buyer needs to review: installation environment, material/finish match, physical or control sample, production/QC reference, packing and installation protection, and maintenance handover when relevant.
- Use a sequence that follows the customer's risk: environment → physical reference → production/QC → installation and care. Do not narrate workshop activity that does not change a customer decision.
- Connect each company-specific statement to evidence in `sources.md`. A product or sample page proves that the option exists; it does not prove an unnamed project, performance result, or universal company practice.

### 3. Physical-reference caveat when the topic is finish-sensitive

- State clearly that screen images cannot reliably establish gloss, texture, patina depth, grain, reflection, or stone variation when those attributes matter.
- Describe a physical/control sample as the approval reference before production, then list the fields that should be recorded: substrate/system, preparation and finishing sequence, approved visual range, treatment of seams/edges/transitions, acceptable variation, coating or topcoat, and cleaning/inspection/repair points.
- Add the limitation that a small flat sample is a control, not a guarantee that reflection or color will read identically across a large curved form.
- Do not introduce a maintenance interval, material grade, repair promise, or other specific operational commitment unless current Origin evidence or an authoritative source supports it. Prefer `inspection points` or `relevant procedures` when the exact interval or scope is not verified.

### 4. Calibrated wording for company-process claims

- Use practical verbs: `review`, `approve`, `record`, `compare`, `protect`, and `identify`.
- Describe QC with representative, observable checks—surface preparation, welds, seams, grain direction, edges, recesses, coating coverage, and representative areas—only when supported by evidence.
- Use calibrated language for evidence-backed process descriptions: `may cover`, `supports review`, and `when variation is critical` are preferable to blanket guarantees.
- Keep the distinction between a final photo/video review and a physical reference: digital review can support review before packing, but does not replace the physical reference when variation matters.
- End the process section with the buyer's next action or approval decision, not a generic brand claim.

## Revision checklist

Before accepting a draft in these topics, ask:

1. Does the title name the actual materials or decision being compared?
2. Is the Origin section about a real customer control point rather than a reusable “why choose us” paragraph?
3. Does the sequence cover environment, physical reference, production/QC, and installation/care only where relevant?
4. Are limitations and natural variation stated where a sample or finish could be misunderstood as a guarantee?
5. Does every Origin-specific statement have matching evidence, and are unsupported intervals, grades, outcomes, or promises removed?
6. Does the section tell the reader what to approve, record, inspect, or ask next?
